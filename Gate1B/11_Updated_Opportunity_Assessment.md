## 3. Reading — what the evidence supports and does not
- **No evidence makes either candidate the winner.** C1 has stronger documented pain; C2 has cleaner rule-checkability. Demand and WTP are unknown for both, prevalence unknown for C2, and general-AI parity unknown for both.
- The earlier tilt toward C1 came from data availability. The FY25 numbers show that *most* health claims are paid and grievance incidence is low relative to claims — that cuts against treating "claim denial" as a mass-market recurring need, while the high stake per case still supports an episodic, high-urgency product.
- C2's strength is mostly **regulatory-determinism**, not demonstrated consumer need. It could be a better *engine proof* and a weaker *business* — or the reverse.
- Challengers (rental, subscriptions, other) stay open pending interviews; nothing here rules them out.
