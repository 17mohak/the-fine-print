# A. DOMAIN DOSSIERS

## A1. Health & general insurance

**Legal architecture.** Insurance Act 1938 (amended Dec 2025 [S2]) → IRDAI regulations (Policyholder Protection Regs 2024 [S1]; legacy 2017 regs) → IRDAI Health Master Circular 29 May 2024 [S2] → insurer product filings → the policy document. Contract law and Consumer Protection Act 2019 sit underneath.

**Rights and obligations commonly relevant**
- **Free look:** 15 days (30 for electronic/distance) under the 2017 regs [S1]; 2024 regs reportedly 30 days, scope disputed (life only vs life+health) [S1]. Not applicable to renewals or porting [S1].
- **Cashless:** insurer to decide pre-auth within 1 hour and discharge authorisation within 3 hours of hospital request [S2].
- **Moratorium:** 5 years (down from 8) after which claims generally cannot be repudiated for non-disclosure/misstatement [S2]; fraud is typically carved out [K].
- **Repudiation:** must be reasoned by reference to specific policy terms and approved by an internal committee [S1].
- **Claim timelines/interest on delay:** exist in the Policyholder regs [K] — figures not verified.
- **Renewal/portability/waiting periods:** PED waiting cap reportedly 3 years [S1/K]; portability rights [K]; renewal cannot be refused merely because a claim was made [K].
- **Policyholder duties:** full disclosure at proposal, timely claim intimation, premium payment, compliance with claim documentation, notify changes [K].

**Required disclosures** [K unless noted]: Customer Information Sheet, benefit illustration/policy wording in prescribed form, standard definitions, list of exclusions, claim procedure, grievance details. Proposal-form accuracy is the **consumer's** disclosure burden.

**Clauses with regulatory significance:** waiting periods (initial/specific/PED), standard-definition exclusions, sub-limits and room-rent proportionate deductions, co-pay, non-payable items list, moratorium interplay, claim-intimation deadlines, free-look, grace period, cancellation/refund. **Whether a given policy clause is enforceable or contrary to IRDAI standardisation is exactly the kind of judgment the product must not make** — it can only flag "differs from standard/regulatory baseline, verify."

**Dispute path:** see §D1.

**State dependence:** low for core insurance (central subject); consumer forum location and local Ombudsman office are geographic.

**Where a pan-India engine can mislead:** product-specific wording varies enormously by insurer and by product version/date; endorsements and riders change the contract; a policy "issued before" or "renewed after" a regulatory change may follow different rules. **The effective-date logic is the hardest problem in this domain.**

## A2. Residential rental / tenancy

**Legal architecture.** Transfer of Property Act 1882 ss.105–117 [K], Registration Act 1908 s.17 [K], state stamp law [K], **state rent control/tenancy statutes** [K], Model Tenancy Act 2021 (advisory; only effective where a state enacts) [S2], plus local police-verification and society rules.

**Rights and obligations (generic, subject to state law)**
- Lease >1 year requires registration; shorter often executed as 11-month agreements (commonly leave-and-licence in some states) [K]. Consequences of non-registration/under-stamping differ by state and by licence-vs-lease characterisation [K].
- Security deposit: **cap and refund rules are state-dependent.** Model Act: 2 months' rent residential [S2], but this binds nobody unless enacted.
- Notice to quit/termination periods, rent-increase mechanisms, repairs, eviction grounds, subletting, lock-in — [K], state-specific.
- Tenant duties: rent, use for permitted purpose, no unauthorised alterations, give possession. Landlord duties: quiet enjoyment, essential repairs, no forcible eviction/utility cutoff [K].

**Disclosures required:** essentially none comparable to financial regulation. Tenant's protection is mainly in statute and the agreement itself.

**Regulatory-significance clauses:** deposit amount/refund timeline, lock-in/notice penalties, rent escalation, lock-in with forfeiture, maintenance/society charges, entry rights, police verification, registration/stamping recital, jurisdiction/arbitration clause.

**Dispute path:** §D3. No unified regulator. Consumer-forum applicability to landlord-tenant is doubtful [K] → legal question Q-T1.

**State dependence: very high.** This is the domain where a pan-India product is **least** safe making conclusions.

## A3. Consumer lending

**Legal architecture.** RBI directions on Fair Practices, KFS, digital lending, penal charges, floating-rate resets, document return, CIC compensation [K/S1] · Digital Lending Directions 2025 [S1] · RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme 2026 (from 1 Jul 2026) [S2] · Consumer Protection Act · SARFAESI/DRT for secured loans [K] · Credit Information Companies Act [K].

**Rights**
- **Key Fact Statement before agreement execution**, incl. APR and charges [S1].
- **Cooling-off exit** in digital loans (principal + interest for the period; reasonable processing fee) — minimum period conflicts across sources (1 vs 3 days) [S1].
- Return of original property documents within a set period after closure with per-day compensation [K]; CIC update delay compensation [K]; no foreclosure/prepayment charge on certain floating-rate retail loans [K — verify date, reported 2026].
- Penal charges rather than penal *interest*, not compounded [K].
- Recovery-conduct norms [K].

**Regulatory-significance clauses:** APR vs headline rate, processing/foreclosure/prepayment fees, penal charges, floating-rate reset and tenor-vs-EMI choice, insurance bundling, LSP/DLA fees, consent for data/credit-limit increases, auto-debit mandate, acceleration/default clauses, arbitration clauses.

**Dispute path:** §D2. **Matters outside RBI regulation:** unregulated/informal lenders, some fintech arrangements, state money-lender acts — remedy path differs and RBI Ombudsman may not apply (L9 unresolved).

**State dependence:** moderate — stamp duty on loan/security documents, money-lender laws, local DRT/court.

## A4. Warranties, subscriptions, service agreements

**Legal architecture.** Consumer Protection Act 2019 [K] · Jurisdiction Rules 2021 [S2] · E-Commerce Rules 2020 [K] · CCPA Dark Patterns Guidelines 2023 [S2] · Sale of Goods Act, Contract Act [K] · RBI e-mandate rules for recurring payments [K] · sector-specific regulators (telecom, electricity, aviation) out of scope.

**Rights:** defect/deficiency remedies (repair, replace, refund, compensation) [K]; unfair contract and unfair trade practice concepts [K]; platform grievance officer and timelines [K]; protection from dark patterns incl. subscription traps [S2]; recurring-payment pre-debit notification and authentication [K].

**Important structural point:** a manufacturer's/retailer's **warranty card is a contract, but statutory rights under CPA and Sale of Goods Act operate independently** and cannot necessarily be excluded by warranty wording [K → Q-W1]. The product must not equate "outside warranty" with "no remedy".

**Regulatory-significance clauses:** warranty duration/start date, exclusions (liquid, misuse), void-if-opened, service-centre-only repair, auto-renewal and cancellation terms, refund policy, unilateral variation, limitation of liability, forum/arbitration clauses.

**Dispute path:** §D4.

# D. GRIEVANCE ESCALATION MAPS

Legend: **[Internal]** entity's own mechanism · **[Regulator]** administrative/ombudsman · **[Statutory forum]** adjudication · **[Court]**. Windows and figures carry confidence tags; none verified against the primary text.

## D1. Insurance
1. **[Internal]** Written complaint to insurer's grievance cell/GRO (Bima Bharosa for IRDAI-tracked complaints). Insurer response expected ~14–15 days [S1] (IRDAI handbook says 2 weeks).
2. **[Regulator]** IRDAI complaint (Bima Bharosa). IRDAI handles supervision; it does **not** adjudicate individual claim entitlement [K].
3. **[Regulator, quasi-adjudicatory]** Insurance Ombudsman (Rules 2017 [S2]): after rejection or no reply (reported 1 month) [S1]; file within 1 year [S1]; award reported ~3 months [S1]; free [S1]; **monetary ceiling unresolved (₹30L / ₹50L)**; complainant must accept award in writing within 30 days [S1]; likely cannot be pursued simultaneously with court/commission proceedings on the same matter [K — verify].
4. **[Statutory forum]** District/State/National Commission by consideration paid [S2]; 2-year limitation [K].
5. **[Court]** Civil court / writ in limited cases [K].
*Condition to model:* ombudsman route has monetary and subject limits (e.g., excludes group/others?) [unknown] → counsel.

## D2. Lending
1. **[Internal]** Bank/NBFC grievance channel → Nodal/Principal Nodal Officer. RE gets reported 30 days [S2 for 2021 scheme].
2. **[Regulator]** RBI Integrated Ombudsman via CMS portal. Scheme **2021 (pre-1 Jul 2026 complaints) vs 2026 (after)** [S2]. 2026: reported 90-day window, 15-day RE reply, ₹30L/₹3L caps [S1]. 2021: 1-year window, ₹20L/₹1L [S2].
3. **[Regulator]** Appellate authority (RBI ED) within 30 days of award/rejection [S2 for 2021].
4. **[Statutory forum]** Consumer Commission (loan = service in most circumstances [K]). **[Court/DRT]** for debt recovery by lender (SARFAESI/DRT) [K].
*Condition:* the same issue cannot be simultaneously before a court/forum (scheme exclusion) [K].
*Unregulated lenders:* RBI route likely unavailable → police/state money-lender authorities/civil court [K, Q-L2].

## D3. Tenancy
1. **[Internal]** Notice/communication to landlord, society/RWA.
2. **[Regulator]** None unified. Where a state has a Rent Authority/Controller (MTA-aligned states or older acts) — [state-specific].
3. **[Statutory forum]** Rent Controller/Tribunal/Court under state law; civil court (recovery of deposit, possession).
4. **[Other]** Lok Adalat / mediation; police only for criminal conduct (e.g., illegal lockout) [K].
*Condition:* forum is determined by state statute and whether the premises fall in rent-control scope [K].

## D4. Warranties/subscriptions/services
1. **[Internal]** Brand/seller grievance officer (mandatory for e-commerce under 2020 Rules [K]).
2. **[Facilitation]** National Consumer Helpline (1915) pre-litigation; CCPA complaints for systemic unfair trade practices [K].
3. **[Statutory forum]** Consumer Commissions (e-filing portal — current system to be verified); mediation under CPA [K].
4. **[Payment channel]** Bank/PSP dispute for unauthorised/recurring debits [K].
*Where not a "consumer" dispute (e.g., commercial purpose):* forum may be unavailable [K].

# F. PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

**Document interpretation**
1. **Effective-date-aware reasoning.** Every regulatory baseline is tied to a (policy issue/renewal/loan execution/complaint) date; the engine must pick the rule version in force on that date or declare "unknown." (Evidence: 2026 Ombudsman scheme, 2024 health circular, Dec 2025 Insurance Act amendment.)
2. **Three-layer output**: (a) what the document says (quote + location), (b) the regulatory baseline relevant (cite + version + date + verification status), (c) the gap/flag. Never merge (b) into (a).
3. **Domain modules need curated rule libraries**, not generic prompting — standard definitions, waiting-period caps, KFS fields, etc., each with an owner and review date.
4. **Fail closed on state-dependent topics:** if state/forum unknown → ask; if unmapped → say we can't determine.
5. **Never infer missing clauses**; "silent document" is reported as unknown, with the baseline shown separately.

**Warnings & boundaries**
6. Standing banner: informational research, not legal advice; no outcome guarantee. Triggers for "consult a lawyer": fraud allegations, high-value claims, eviction/possession, criminal elements, cross-forum or limitation-edge cases.
7. Do not state a rejection is "wrongful/illegal"; use "appears inconsistent with X / worth challenging because Y — insurer may respond Z."
8. Do not present timelines as entitlements guaranteed; say "regulator expects".

**Provenance**
9. Each regulatory fact stores: instrument, section, effective/amended date, source URL, **verification status (S/P)**, last-checked date, reviewer.
10. User-facing evidence chips: Document fact / Regulatory fact / Interpretation / Assumption / Unknown (per charter).
11. A **regulatory change-log service** with alerts; documents re-evaluated when a baseline changes.

**Escalation guidance**
12. Show the ladder with **conditions** (waiting windows, limitation, can't run parallel forums) and compute a **deadline** only with source and confidence.
13. Prepare the complaint pack (chronology, evidence, clause citations); user submits; never state "submitted" unless confirmed (charter). Don't assume portal APIs.
14. Evidence capture for dark-pattern/subscription cases (screens, cancellation attempts) is high-value and low-legal-risk.

**Privacy/compliance of the product itself:** DPDP Act obligations (consent, purpose limitation, security, deletion) on health and financial documents; retention and breach plans [K, P-TODO].

**Suggested sequencing signal (hypothesis, not a recommendation):** insurance and lending have central, documented regimes and the most automatable ladder; tenancy is state-fragmented and weakest for reliable pan-India interpretation; subscriptions/warranties have a clear statutory base but heterogeneous documents.

# G. UNRESOLVED LEGAL QUESTIONS FOR QUALIFIED INDIAN COUNSEL

**Insurance**
- Q-I1: Exact scope/commencement of 30-day free look (life vs health; 2017 vs 2024 regs).
- Q-I2: Impact of the Dec 2025 amendments on s.45, policyholder protection, and IRDAI powers; commencement dates.
- Q-I3: Insurance Ombudsman monetary ceiling and exclusions; interplay with consumer forum and court; non-compliance penalties.
- Q-I4: Enforceability of exclusions inconsistent with IRDAI standardisation; contra proferentem authorities; treatment of proportionate deductions.
- Q-I5: Whether the 5-year moratorium is retrospective for older policies/continuous renewals.
- Q-I6: Claim-timeline and interest figures under current regs.

**Tenancy**
- Q-T1: Is a landlord–tenant dispute a "consumer" dispute at all? Case law.
- Q-T2: Enforceability of lock-in/penalty and deposit-forfeiture clauses under TPA/Contract Act (s.74) per state.
- Q-T3: Legal effect of unregistered/under-stamped 11-month agreements in each launch state.
- Q-T4: Current MTA adoption/state laws for launch states.

**Lending**
- Q-L1: Cooling-off minimum (1 vs 3 days) and fee treatment in the Digital Lending Directions 2025.
- Q-L2: Remedies against unregulated lenders/loan apps; status of any central ban legislation.
- Q-L3: Exact 2026 Ombudsman scheme windows, 30-day pre-condition, forum-parallelism bar, transitional rules.
- Q-L4: Prepayment/foreclosure restrictions — scope, date, entities.
- Q-L5: Enforceability of unilateral-variation and arbitration clauses in loan agreements.

**Warranty/subscription**
- Q-W1: Can warranty terms limit statutory remedies? Scope of "unfair contract" powers.
- Q-W2: Cancellation/refund rights for subscriptions under dark-pattern guidelines — enforcement route for individuals.
- Q-W3: Consumer forum e-filing system currently in use and procedural rules.

**Product-level**
- Q-P1: Does generating complaint drafts or "interpretations" constitute legal practice under the Advocates Act or Bar Council rules? Safe design boundary.
- Q-P2: DPDP compliance for health/financial document processing; cross-border processing.
- Q-P3: Liability and disclaimer design; consumer-protection obligations on the product's own recommendations (commission-free principle).
- Q-P4: Legality of any interaction with official grievance portals on users' behalf (terms of use, authentication).
